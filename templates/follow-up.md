# Follow-up — exactly one, then stop

Sent **6 days** after the original. Three lines. Adds something new. No guilt, no
"just bumping this," no "I wanted to float this to the top of your inbox."

**Never a third follow-up.** A third is how you become a story founders tell each other.

## Shape

```
<reply in the same thread — do not start a new one>

Hi <first name> —

<1 sentence: something NEW. Not a repeat. A thing they shipped, a hire they made,
 a piece of news, or one more concrete idea.>

<1 sentence: the ask, smaller than last time.>

Jordan
```

**Hard limit: 45 words.**

## Worked example

> Hi Maya —
>
> Saw you shipped the Xero connector this week — that closes the gap I mentioned on the
> import step.
>
> Still happy to talk if a generalist ends up on your list. If not, no hard feelings and good
> luck with the build.
>
> Jordan

## Why the exit line matters

*"If not, no hard feelings"* does three things: it removes the obligation, it signals he
isn't going to keep pestering, and it makes replying feel low-cost. Founders reply to people
who are easy to say no to.

## Banned
- ~~"Just bumping this up"~~ / ~~"Following up on my last email"~~ / ~~"Circling back"~~
- ~~"I know you're busy, but..."~~
- ~~Re-pasting the original pitch~~
- ~~Any hint of being owed a reply~~

## Rules
- Same thread, always. A new thread reads as a second cold email.
- If they replied at all — even "not right now" — this template **does not apply**. Reply
  like a human, thank them, and ask to stay in touch.
- After this one: mark the company `closed` in `ledger/companies.jsonl`. Done forever.
